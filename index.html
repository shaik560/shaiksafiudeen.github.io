About

The story behind the screens

I'm a UI/UX designer based in Nagapattinam, Tamil Nadu, one year and change into a career built on a simple belief: good design is invisible until it isn't there. My job is to make sure people never have to think about the interface — only about what they came to do.

I started out as a Computer Science & Business Systems student, which means I came into design already comfortable with how software actually gets built. That background shapes how I work — I don't hand off static mockups and hope for the best, I design with implementation, edge cases, and data states in mind from the first wireframe.

My first real design job was freelance: a food court ordering system that needed to work for people scanning a menu on their phone mid-queue. That project taught me the lesson that's stuck with me since — constraints make better designers than freedom does. From there I moved into an agency-style role at Zuntra Digital, then Coders Boutique, shipping client work across five-plus projects, and I currently design product experiences at Femtosoft Technologies.

Outside of client work, I use self-directed projects like StudyCoach AI and BuildQuick to explore harder problems — AI-assisted planning, B2B workflows — that don't usually show up in agency briefs. It's how I keep growing past whatever a given job asks of me.

CuriosityClarity over clevernessFast iterationHonest feedbackOwnership

Research before pixels

I don't open Figma until I understand who's using the thing and what they're trying to get done. Assumptions get tested, not shipped.

Systems, not screens

Every interface I design is built from a component system underneath — consistent spacing, typography, and states — so it scales instead of splintering.

Design that ships

My CS background means I design with real constraints in mind: loading states, empty states, error states, and how components actually map to code.

StudyCoach AI

FigmaUX ResearchAI/UX
Product Designer (Solo)
Overview

An AI-driven career and study planning product built for Indian college students juggling coursework, competitive exams, and career uncertainty at the same time.

Problem

Students had access to endless study resources but no single place that adapted to their actual schedule, skill gaps, or burnout risk — most planning tools assumed unlimited time and motivation.

Process
  • Interviewed students across engineering and arts colleges to map real weekly routines, not idealized ones.
  • Mapped a skill-gap detection flow that compares a student's stated goals against their current progress.
  • Prototyped a burnout-aware planner that adjusts workload suggestions based on self-reported energy, not just deadlines.
  • Ran informal usability passes with 6 students to catch friction in onboarding and daily check-ins.
Solution

A guided onboarding that builds a personalised roadmap in under 3 minutes, plus a daily planner that re-balances itself when a student falls behind instead of just piling on guilt.

BuildQuick

FigmaMobile UIB2B
UI/UX Designer
Overview

A 13-screen B2B mobile app for construction material procurement, designed for site managers ordering materials from job sites, not offices.

Problem

Site managers were placing bulk orders over phone calls and WhatsApp, with no reliable way to track delivery status or verify who was authorized to approve an order.

Process
  • Shadowed the procurement workflow to understand where phone-call ordering broke down.
  • Designed an OTP-secured approval flow so only authorized site managers could confirm large orders.
  • Built a real-time delivery tracking screen prioritizing status clarity over visual complexity — usable in bright outdoor light.
  • Structured bulk ordering around material categories site managers already used mentally, not a generic e-commerce grid.
Solution

A focused 13-screen flow covering browse → bulk order → OTP approval → live tracking, designed for one-handed use on a job site with a cracked-screen mid-range phone in mind.

The Spot

FigmaVisual Design
UI Designer
Overview

A digital menu interface for a food court, replacing a cluttered printed board with a browsable, appetite-driving screen and print-ready layout.

Problem

The original printed menu buried popular items among dozens of categories, and had no digital counterpart for in-app or kiosk ordering.

Process
  • Audited the existing printed menu to identify which categories actually drove orders.
  • Reorganized items into clearer categories, with visual hierarchy prioritizing bestsellers.
  • Designed a layout that works identically well on a kiosk screen and as a printable PDF, avoiding two separate design efforts.
Solution

A clean, appetite-forward menu system with consistent visual rules for categorisation, pricing, and imagery — built once, deployed to screen and print.
